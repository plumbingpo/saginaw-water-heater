How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Hot water running out before everyone has showered
  • Big-tub or multi-shower demand the current heater cannot meet
  • Remodel plans that add fixtures or a second bathroom
  • Missing code items like an expansion tank, drain pan, or proper venting
  • Plans to change fuel types or go all-electric
  • Taps at the far end of the house that take minutes to warm up
  • A water heater installed above finished rooms with no drain pan
  • A remodel that moves the utility space to a new spot

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

Installing a water heater from scratch pulls in gas, venting, electrical, and plumbing work all at once. Gas lines need sizing for the whole system's load, plus a leak test. A vent outside the manufacturer's limits on length, diameter, slope, or clearance can put carbon monoxide into living space. Electric, tankless, and heat pump heaters need circuits that match their load, sometimes more than an older panel can supply. The plumbing side needs a correctly rated relief valve and a full-size discharge line, and depending on location, code may require an expansion tank, a drain pan, seismic straps, or a permit and inspection.

What you get from a pro is an installation that is done once. Our technicians plan the gas line, vent, and circuit before the first cut, take care of the permit when your jurisdiction requires one, and install the relief valve discharge and code items correctly. We test every joint, check every gas install for draft and carbon monoxide, and set the temperature low enough to prevent scalding. Hurried installs leave behind undersized gas lines, poor vent pitch, and missing expansion tanks. Ours are done to code and to last. How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • A 120-degree setting protects against burns and slows mineral buildup
  • Have the anode rod inspected every few years to protect the tank
  • Glance at the pan and discharge pipe every month or two; water there means it is time to call
  • Drain a few gallons from a new tank twice a year to control sediment
  • Nothing flammable belongs near a gas burner or its air supply
  • Plan an annual descaling if you installed a tankless unit
  • Leave working space around the heater, and never stack storage against it
