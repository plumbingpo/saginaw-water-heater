How Bay City Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Routine maintenance makes all the difference between a water heater that runs for years and one that fails early. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode inspection every few years, a quick relief valve test once a year, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The serial number on the label usually encodes the manufacture date, and its age says a lot about what comes next. Most tank models reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units about 20. If your heater is past that range and is also showing rusty water, rumbling, or dampness under the tank, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than an emergency replacement after a leak.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Take a spent anode rod: replacing it is a quick, inexpensive repair, but once it's gone, the water begins corroding the tank itself, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and spares you a flooded utility room.
