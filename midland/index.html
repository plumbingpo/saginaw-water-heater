Keeping Your Midland Water Heater Running Longer

A little routine care makes all the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode rod check every three to five years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

How old is your water heater? The rating plate on the tank usually includes the month and year it was built, and its age says a lot about what comes next. Most tank heaters reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units about 20. If your heater is beyond that range and is also showing rusty water, rumbling, or moisture at the base, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement after a leak.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet the same sediment, neglected for a few years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and burn out the lower element on an electric model, forcing a replacement much sooner. Save our number in your contacts today so help is one call away the moment something seems off. A quick call saves time, money, and cold showers.
